# 1. The conversation, recorded

**Where eleven spikes and three research rounds leave the project.** The mechanism works: one decode gives an embedding and a grammar-constrained op list; the contract and validator hold; serving and pipeline order are measured. Accuracy on both jobs is a training result: prompting helped only the 4B, scale alone helped nothing, and 147 seconds of adapter training moved the 2B's retrieval further than anything else. The two jobs fight inside one adapter: contrastive alone or with a joint loss kills op emission; a KL anchor keeps emission alive at a retrieval cost; a per-pass toggle keeps both at their separate best with one weight set (011: K01 0.569 and K10 0.621 MRR with emission alive; R 0.708 and J 0.738 with emission dead; T toggle 0.708 plus base generation; A embedder 0.801).

**Is full fine-tuning required?** Not on the evidence. Full fine-tuning forgets more than low-rank adapters, needs memory the 16 GB card does not have for a 2B in full precision with optimizer state, and the labs' small-model recipes freeze the backbone and train per-job adapters. The spikes show the recipe and the data were the limits, not adapter capacity: the low-capacity arm proved a small update leaves generation alone, and every retrieval gain came from 15 training documents. Full fine-tuning is the escalation if a properly trained adapter plateaus, and GaLore-style full-plasticity under a memory cap comes before true full fine-tuning.

**What a proper dataset changes.** The retrieval near-miss (0.72 to 0.75 against 0.80) is a generalisation gap from 15 documents with train MRR at 0.95: too little data, and no spike has addressed it. Research 3's recipe: teacher op traces and query-passage pairs over a representative corpus, evolution operators, staged filtering with Proof as the final gate, exact and semantic dedup, about one in eight sections with an empty op list so the model learns to abstain, whole-document held-out splits.

**The training path, in order.** (1) Two adapters with the toggle as the baseline; it ships as a two-pass v0.1 if nothing better lands. (2) The KL-anchored single adapter, longer and at higher rank, warm-started from few-shot SFT on the op data, as the one-pass candidate. (3) Distill before any RL: Proof-filtered rejection sampling as the control for every RL arm; RL against Proof only after Proof's precision is measured. (4) Re-run the size sweep: trained 2B versus trained 4B is open; D-MS-05 stays a rig decision.

**Model size and precision.** A 1B in bf16 is about 2 GB of weights (MiniCPM5-1B about 2 GB bf16, 1.1 GB Q8; MiniCPM5-2B about 4 GB bf16, 2.3 GB Q8). Full fine-tuning a 1B in bf16 needs roughly 2 GB weights, 2 GB gradients, 4 to 8 GB AdamW state, so 8 to 12 GB before activations: realistic at 1B, marginal at 2B. LoRA on the 1B needs the 2 GB frozen weights plus tens of megabytes; 011's 2B runs peaked at 7 to 10 GB. Precision recipe: bf16 for weights and forward and backward, fp32 for optimizer state and master weights (standard mixed precision, what 011's venv already does through autocast). Not fp16 (small exponent range, loss scaling) and not fp32 throughout (double memory, half speed, no accuracy gain). Precision was never a measured limit: the 2B hit a data limit, and Q8 applies only to inference where 003 measured no loss worth chasing. A trained 1B is an unmeasured point on the size curve (003 found the untrained 1B unusable), so a full fine-tune of the 1B is one arm next to the anchored 2B adapter, not instead of it.

**Spike 012 as first drafted.** One spike with arms on identical data and identical evaluation, separating dataset size, adapter versus full fine-tune, and 1B versus 2B: build the dataset to a document budget the owner sets; train the anchored 2B adapter, the two-adapter toggle, the full fine-tune of MiniCPM5-1B in bf16 with fp32 optimizer state, optionally a 1B adapter; score with one gated joint metric (test MRR against the 0.6B embedder, op emission under the grammar through the 003 harness after GGUF conversion, syntax versus grounding split); report Proof-pass and MRR against dataset size. Pass condition stated up front: an arm reaches MRR within 0.05 of the embedder with Proof-pass above the untrained 2B's on held-out documents. Whichever arm passes is MelodyScribe-{size}-v0.1. One owner decision before launch: the teacher document budget.

**The correction that reshapes it: train to the harness, not hypothetically.** Everything trained so far used spike-local approximations: 006 and 011 scored generation with a grammar-free HF decode of a bare prompt, retrieval targets were one-line teacher questions against whole documents, and no arm produced the runtime's actual Score prompt or its actual op list under the actual grammar. Training on the harness means examples byte-identical to what the runtime feeds the model and targets that the runtime accepts: inputs are compiled Scores from 001's compiler over real documents in 009's instruction-first order; emission targets are Proof-passing v0.2 op lists produced by the teacher through the same compiler and validator, including empty lists, scored under the runtime grammar through the 003 harness after GGUF conversion; embedding targets come from the harness's retrieval path scored through 010's stack against the 0.6B embedder; the recall-time step ([RQ] continuation and the payload from 010's format) is trained too; evaluation is 010's end-to-end run over held-out documents. Folio and agent ops enter targets only when the harness can validate them, which today means graph and SQL. The prerequisite this exposes: the compiler, Proof, the v0.2 grammar, the retrieval stack, and the payload assembler live in four spike folders and must be assembled into one importable harness module that data generation and evaluation both call. That module is the seed of the real build.

# 2. What deep research 4 adds (2026-09-13, after the conversation)

The harness-frameworks note settled the harness shape before the fine-tune, which is what the conversation asked for. Deltas that change the spike plan:

1. **The dataset is harness-shaped, so the harness module comes first.** Spike 012 is now the harness module plus the dataset; training moves to spike 013 on that dataset. Training against an approximation would repeat 006 and 011.
2. **Abstention is a first-class target.** Explicit empty-op sections and out-of-scope sections (Hammer 2410.04587; lane 1 implication 6), not only "one in eight sections empty".
3. **Residual-only routing.** Deterministic pre-gates decide code (tree-sitter) and SQL-shaped (date, number, table cell) before the model sees the section (lanes 2, 7, 9). The dataset records, per teacher op, whether a rule would have routed it, so 012 reports the residual share the model actually has to decide. The v0.2 schema stays; lifecycle ops (ADD, UPDATE, INVALIDATE, NOOP) and validity columns are a v0.3 schema decision for the owner, not something 012 changes silently.
4. **Thinking off.** No thinking data in the dataset; targets are non-thinking single-pass emission (lane 4).
5. **Runtime decoder is the judge.** Every trained arm is merged, converted to GGUF, and scored under the llama.cpp grammar through the harness module; HF grammar-free decodes are diagnostics only (lane 1 implication 3; 011 deviation closed).
6. **Rewards later, staged.** RL waits for 015; when it comes, stage 1 rewards grammar-valid emission and invocation with no answer component, stage 2 rewards correctness, non-model tokens masked, Proof rule-based with hack probes (lane 10). Spike 012 adds Proof hack-probes so Proof's precision is measured before it becomes a reward.
7. **Evaluation shape.** pass^k over repeated trials, AST plus execution grading with an irrelevance split, syntax versus grounding, and capacity under a latency SLO where serving is measured (lane 10).

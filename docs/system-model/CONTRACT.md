# Fitting Contract

**Status: normative.** This document governs the fitting between the machine and any ported modality. `.planning/architectures/D-VARIANTS/SELECTION.md` — including its spike-005 AMENDMENT — is the frozen source this document transcribes and elaborates; where this document and SELECTION.md disagree, **SELECTION.md governs**. The architecture selection itself (D4 One Machine: one machine, no sandbox regime, no `regime` field in the contract) is **not re-litigated here** — it is only stated and built upon. This document **supersedes** the contract skeleton at SELECTION.md lines 135–198 as the normative artifact a builder implements against; SELECTION.md remains the record of the reasoning that produced it.

## Reading order

This document is long enough that no reader needs all of it on a first pass. Start with `## Conventions` and `## §0` regardless of role — they fix the tag vocabulary and naming convention every later section assumes — then jump to the sections your job actually touches:

- **A component author** starts at `## §0` (naming and versioning) and `## §13` (the socket type system a new component's ports must satisfy).
- **A part author** starts at `## §2` (node kinds and effects), `## §13` (socket compatibility), and `## §14` (the capability vocabulary a part declares against).
- **A consumer developer** (building against the machine's public surface — Sourcerer applets, the CLI, or any other client) starts at `## §18` (the public seam).
- **A machine implementer** starts at `## §3` (depth and containment) and `## §7` through `## §11` (registries, opaque-node admission, budget, versioned instruments, plane rules).

`## Ruling R-1`, `## §15`, `## §16`, and `## §17` are worked answers to owner-raised or open questions and reward reading in full regardless of role. `## Appendix A`, `## Appendix B`, and `## Appendix C` are audit tools, not reading-order stops — consult them when checking a specific clause's source, a specific requirement's coverage, or a specific section's falsifier, not on a first pass through the document.

## §0 — Component naming and versioning

**Satisfies:** CONTRACT-08

A component name MUST be of the form `namespace/name`. Both segments MUST be lowercase kebab-case. Examples: `core/chunker-token`, `lightrag/entity-extractor`. [docs-verified] [CONTEXT D-01]

The namespace names the component's owner. A name MUST be unique within its namespace; the same `name` segment MAY be reused across different namespaces, in which case the two components are unrelated. [docs-verified] [CONTEXT D-01]

**Namespace registry.** The namespace set is fixed and enumerated here, not open-ended:

| Namespace | Owner |
|---|---|
| `core` | Machine-owned primitives |
| `lightrag` | The LightRAG port |
| `hipporag` | The HippoRAG 2 port |
| *(further namespaces)* | Added one per ported system, as systems are ported |

Creating a namespace is a contract-level change tied to the porting protocol (PARTS-07). It MUST NOT happen implicitly at wiring-authoring time — a wiring author cannot mint a new namespace by writing one into a wiring file. [docs-verified] [CONTEXT D-02]

**Version string.** The version segment of `name@version` is semver (`MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`), and the two version axes map onto its three numbers by a fixed rule: **MAJOR carries the interface axis** — a MAJOR bump means the component's socket or capability surface changed. **MINOR and PATCH carry the implementation axis** — the component's internal behavior changed (algorithm, prompt, weights, bug fix) without changing what it accepts, emits, or declares. [docs-verified] [CONTEXT D-03]

The consumer-facing consequence follows directly: a MAJOR bump MAY break a `deps` edge that wires to this component, because the interface it presents has changed. A MINOR or PATCH bump MUST NOT break a `deps` edge — any wiring that validated against the old MINOR/PATCH MUST still validate against the new one. [inference] [CONTEXT D-03]

**Version minting.** An experiment arm MUST NOT mint a version. An arm is identified by its delta (the merge-patch or JSON-Patch applied to the base) plus its `config_hash` — not by a semver string. A semver version is minted only at **promotion**, the atomic alias repoint that makes an arm the active pointer. [docs-verified] [CONTEXT D-04]

This is the same rule as the ledger/projection split stated elsewhere in this contract: running an arm never appends to the promotion ledger, so an arm that is never promoted never acquires a version — it remains an unversioned trial, identified only by its delta and `config_hash`, until and unless it is promoted. [docs-verified] [CONTEXT D-04] [SELECTION §6]

### §0.4 — Collision, ownership, and retirement

Two components in different namespaces MAY share a `name` segment; the machine MUST treat `a/x` and `b/x` as unrelated components with independent version lines. [inference] [CONTEXT D-01]

A name, once published in the component registry, MUST NOT be reused for a different component — the component registry is byte-sized and never deleted, so retirement is a registry state, not a name release. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §7]

The namespace owner is responsible for uniqueness inside its namespace; the machine MUST refuse to register a second component with the same `namespace/name`. [inference] [CONTEXT D-01]

A ported system's namespace is owned by the port, not by the upstream project; upstream attribution lives in the registry's `upstream_ref` field, not in the name. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §7]

### §0.5 — Rename map for names already in the record

A component name written anywhere in the project record before this contract existed is an illustration, not a name, and MUST be renamed to conform when this contract or any document downstream of it cites it. [docs-verified] [CONTEXT D-05]

The mapping for the names the record already carries — at minimum the four names in the worked wiring JSON (`.claude/skills/spike-findings-rag-graph-vector-raw/references/wiring-spec-and-validation.md:18–30`):

| Name in the record | Source | Conforming name |
|---|---|---|
| `recursive-chunker` | wiring-spec-and-validation.md:18-30 | `core/chunker-recursive` |
| `bge-m3` | wiring-spec-and-validation.md:18-30 | `core/embedder-bge-m3` |
| `hybrid-retriever` | wiring-spec-and-validation.md:18-30 | `core/retriever-hybrid` |
| `crag` | wiring-spec-and-validation.md:18-30 | `core/harness-crag` |

The conforming names are this contract's choice, not a verified prior list (01-RESEARCH.md assumption A3). [inference] [new-synthesis]

§1's worked example and every later section MUST use these conforming names. [inference] [CONTEXT D-05]

**Consistency with the frozen instance tuple.** The naming convention constrains only the `name@version` member of the frozen instance tuple `(name@version, config_hash, resolved_dependency_ids)`; instance identity remains as §1 freezes it — a name is not an instance identity and a node id is not an instance identity. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1]

**Falsifier:** This convention is refuted if two components that a wiring must distinguish cannot be given distinct `namespace/name` strings without inventing a namespace outside the enumerated set, or if the interface/implementation axis split cannot be applied to a real ported component's change history (e.g. a LightRAG port change that is genuinely ambiguous between the two axes). [new-synthesis]

## Ruling R-1 — The `evidence` structural depth

**Satisfies:** CONTRACT-06

### The question.

Once `ItemKind` and machine-resolvable refs (`ChunkRef`, deref-raising) land, does `evidence` remain a distinct structural depth, or does it collapse into "opaque node that passes clause 3 at its boundary"? [docs-verified] [SELECTION §Open questions 7]

### Reading A — `evidence` remains distinct.

The three-value ladder `depth ∈ {opaque, evidence, stage}` is what the validator computes and what the taint rule (effective depth = min over transitive `deps`) minimises over. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §3]

`evidence`-tier propagation is one of the twelve cases spike 005's `taint.py` measured directly — an `evidence`-depth part feeding a stage extractor writing shared is refused, confirming `evidence < stage` behaves correctly as a distinct rung. Removing the rung removes a measured, working behavior, not a hypothetical one. [code-verified] [spike 005]

§4's evidence-item tiers `T0..T3` are machine-stamped from what arrived and read the structural depth rung directly — T2 requires stage-depth producing boundaries with instance hashes. §5's gate policy computes at the "greatest common depth" and its `insufficient-depth` verdict names the missing instrument against a middle rung, not a binary choice. Both sections already read the three-value ladder as written. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §4] [SELECTION §5]

The entire frozen skeleton — §3's depth enum, §4's tier stamping, §5's gate policy — is written in three values; keeping `evidence` distinct is the zero-edit reading against the frozen text. [inference] [new-synthesis]

### Reading B — `evidence` collapses.

With `ItemKind` landing as a typed union (§4) and `ChunkRef` deref-raising rather than silently degrading, a node's evidence-level trustworthiness becomes observable at its boundary — clause 3's `refs_in`/`refs_resolved` count directly measures whether a node's declared evidence resolves. The `evidence` rung may therefore be a derived predicate computed from boundary behavior rather than a structural value the validator must carry through the whole taint computation. [inference] [SELECTION §4]

A two-value structural depth `depth ∈ {opaque, stage}` plus a boundary predicate (e.g. `refs_resolved == refs_in`) is a smaller vocabulary for the validator to compute and for a wiring author to reason about — one fewer rung in the taint minimum, one fewer case in every downstream table that enumerates depth. [inference] [new-synthesis]

SELECTION.md itself frames this open question as "a step-8 simplification question" — the collapse is the possibility the frozen skeleton explicitly invites this phase to evaluate, not a deviation from it. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §Open questions 7]

### What each reading costs.

**Reading A (distinct) costs:** no section is rewritten. §3 keeps `depth ∈ {opaque, evidence, stage}` and the taint rule as frozen. §4 keeps its tier stamping reading the structural rung. §5 keeps `insufficient-depth` naming a genuine middle rung. The machine continues to compute and carry a three-value enum through every taint minimum. The cost is vocabulary size, not rewriting. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**Reading B (collapse) costs:** §3 must restate `depth ∈ {opaque, stage}` and redefine the taint rule's minimum over two values instead of three; it must additionally define the boundary predicate that replaces the `evidence` rung and state where in the pipeline it is evaluated. §4 must restate its tier ladder so that what previously read the `evidence` structural depth instead reads the new boundary predicate — a change to how tiers are machine-stamped, not merely a renaming. §5 must restate "greatest common depth" over two values and redefine what `insufficient-depth` names when there is no middle rung to point at — it becomes a boundary-predicate failure rather than a depth-rung failure. The machine must newly compute and enforce the boundary predicate at every node boundary rather than carrying a value through the taint minimum. [inference] [new-synthesis]

Under Reading B, spike 005's measured `evidence`-tier propagation case (`taint.py` case 9: an `evidence`-depth part feeding a stage extractor writing shared is refused) no longer maps onto the contract as written — that case exercised a structural rung this reading removes. The ruling would owe either a re-run of `taint.py` with the case restated against the two-value model plus the boundary predicate, or an explicit argument that the boundary predicate reproduces the same refusal the structural rung measured. Neither is done in this task. [inference] [new-synthesis]

### A third reading — the two-axis decomposition.

Before ruling, a third reading was raised and considered: the ladder may compress two independent properties into one dimension — (a) structural declaration (opaque ↔ stage: can the machine see inside the node) and (b) output verifiability (are the node's outputs typed, checkable items — the §4 `ItemKind` machinery). These two properties do not always move together. A fully declared, stage-depth LLM extractor can still produce content the `ItemKind` machinery cannot verify (free-text output with no checkable structure). An opaque node — the machine cannot see inside it — can still produce fully schema-checkable output (a black-box parser emitting a well-typed `ItemKind`). [inference] Under this reading, `evidence` is not a rung on a single ladder at all; it is the derived name for one point in a two-axis space — (opaque structural declaration, verified output) — and the contract should compute and gate on the two axes directly rather than name their intersection as a third structural value. [inference]

### The ruling.

`evidence` MUST remain a distinct structural depth: `depth ∈ {opaque, evidence, stage}` stands exactly as frozen at SELECTION §3, unamended by this ruling. [inference] [SELECTION §Open questions 7] [new-synthesis]

Consequences, stated as obligations a later reader can check: §3 MUST continue computing depth over the three-value enum and the taint rule's minimum over transitive `deps` MUST continue to range over three values, unchanged. §4 MUST continue stamping evidence-item tiers `T0..T3` against the structural `evidence` rung exactly as written — no tier definition changes. §5 MUST continue gating at the greatest common depth over three values, and `insufficient-depth` MUST continue to be nameable against the middle rung, not only a binary opaque/stage split. No section written after this ruling may reintroduce a two-value depth enum or a boundary-predicate substitute for the structural `evidence` rung without a superseding ruling. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**Why `distinct` wins today.** Of the three readings — distinct, collapsed, two-axis — `distinct` is the only one that is free now and reversible later: the frozen skeleton (§3–§5 as written) and spike 005's case-9 measurement already use the three-value ordering, unmodified. [code-verified] [spike 005] Both the collapsed reading and the two-axis reading cost a restatement of §3, §4, and §5 plus re-measurement on ground spike 005 never tested (case 9 exercises the structural rung, not a boundary predicate or a two-axis derivation) — a cost paid now for no measured gain. A contract amendment made before any build exists is cheap to make later if the two-axis model is ever measured to reproduce what the three-value ladder already does; the reverse — discovering after a build that the collapsed or two-axis model was needed — is expensive. The verdict is deliberately the reversible one. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**Scope note — black/gray/white-box framing does not justify this ruling.** CONTEXT D-09's black/gray/white-box spectrum (opaque / partial / decomposed entry rungs, all three runnable in the same wiring) describes a property of a *wiring* — a mix of node depths combined by the taint rule's effective-depth-min across the whole graph. It is not a restatement of, or an argument for, the `evidence` rung itself, which is a per-node structural value. Recording this explicitly prevents the gray-box argument being misread as the reason `evidence` stays distinct; the actual reason is stated above (cost and reversibility), not the gray-box spectrum. [inference]

**Falsifier:** this ruling is refuted — and superseded by the two-axis model — if an experiment shows that an `ItemKind`-derived output-verifiability predicate reproduces both (a) spike 005's case-9 refusal behavior (an `evidence`-depth part feeding a stage extractor writing shared is refused) and (b) the §5 gate's `insufficient-depth` tiering, with no case the three-value ladder distinguishes that the two-axis derivation cannot also distinguish. [inference] [new-synthesis]

## §1 — Wiring format and identity

**Satisfies:** CONTRACT-01

A wiring MUST be encoded as JSON with no evaluation semantics — nothing a wiring file contains may be executed as code by the validator or the executor; whatever the format could compute is something the validator would have to sandbox, and JSON's poverty is what keeps that sandbox unnecessary. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1]

A wiring document has five top-level members. `nodes` maps a node id to a component instance; **node ids are positions in the wiring and MUST NOT be treated as instance identities** — two nodes MAY share the same `component@version` with identical config as legitimate fan-out, and a validator that keys nodes by instance identity silently drops one branch, a failure mode measured directly in the sweep behind this rule. [docs-verified] [SELECTION Lineup-wide 9] `deps` is a partial order over named nodes, not a total order by priority; cycles are legal wiring content (a CRAG-style retry loop is a legitimate modality) and the validator MUST report an unintended cycle as data — `{cycle: [...]}` — never as an interpreter fault. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1] `recipe` names the nodes that constitute the index recipe, giving SA-1 (recipe identity) and SA-2 (sub-recipe stamps for chunker, extraction, embedding) a declared source. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1] `harnesses` is an ordered array; harness order MUST be read from array position, never inferred from merge semantics. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1] `provides` names the wiring's output nodes and MUST include at least one node satisfying the universal core (`retrieve` or `answer`). [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1]

**Edge semantics.** An edge MUST be interpreted as exactly-once dataflow per run. Whether fan-out branches execute concurrently is a **declared executor property, not a wiring property**; this setting keys the A/A null the promotion gate calibrates under, so a wiring MUST NOT be read as making a concurrency guarantee it does not state. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1] [SELECTION Lineup-wide 6]

**Identity.** `config_hash` MUST be computed as SHA-256 over the author-supplied input JSON, canonicalised per RFC 8785 (the JSON Canonicalisation Scheme) — never over a tool's normalised output, since re-hashing byte-identical input after a defaulted-option change has already destroyed in-flight A/B baselines under the wrong rule. [code-verified] [SELECTION Lineup-wide 10] Canonicalisation MUST exclude integers outside int64 range, and MUST collapse the int/float distinction so that `1` and `1.0` hash identically; the environment hash is included in `config_hash`. [code-verified] [SELECTION Lineup-wide 10] Component instance identity is the frozen tuple `(name@version, config_hash, resolved_dependency_ids)`. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1] For an opaque node, the environment hash MUST cover the whole resolved runtime closure, not merely the component's own build inputs — a narrower hash would let two differently-provisioned opaque instances collide on identity. [inference] [SELECTION §1] Runtime-minted instances for planner-emitted plan nodes carry no author-supplied `config_hash` to hash, and their identity MUST instead be computed as `H(component_instance_hash, JCS(runtime_config), parent_instance_hash, ordinal)`. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1]

**Cache-partition key — repaired at `PARTS-04` D7.** A node's cache partition key MUST be derivable from its `(name@version, config_hash, resolved_dependency_ids)` tuple plus its declared input values, and MUST NOT include any field outside them. LightRAG's response cache is the measured case forcing this clause: the key is doubly mode-partitioned (`param.mode` inside the args hash, `operate.py:4792-4798`, and as the flattened key prefix, `utils.py:3835`) while the underlying prompt is mode-free (`operate.py:4814-4818`) — under the arm mechanism `mode` leaves the query object entirely, so a wide, non-conformant key would make arms that should share one cache entry partition apart, or share one they should not, with run order becoming load-bearing. This clause is the identity-side statement of the same rule `## §19.2`'s store-mediated self-coupling clause states from the granularity side; a reader finding either MUST find the same requirement. [docs-verified] [PARTS-04 D7]

**Pinning.** A spec pins ranges of contract versions it is compatible with; a per-run lock MUST record the resolved behaviour versions, artifact ids, and the resolved model bound to each role. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1] Artifacts (as distinct from wirings) MUST be content-addressed by a NAR-style hash over the artifact tree. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1]

**Arms.** An arm MUST be expressed as a base wiring plus an RFC 7386 merge-patch for additive and overriding changes; a differing component-kind tag in the patch MUST replace the whole `kind` subtree rather than merge into it, since a partial merge would leave orphaned fields from the previous kind. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1] Merge-patch alone cannot fail-close a removal — RFC 7386 expresses deletion with a `null` sentinel, so an unknown or mistyped key is a silent no-op by construction — so any subtractive change to an arm MUST instead be expressed as an RFC 6902 operation, whose `remove` errors when the target does not exist. [code-verified] [SELECTION §1] The base wiring MUST be independently re-validated with no arms applied, so that an arm's override can never mask a base type error. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1]

**Validator behaviour as contract.** The validator MUST return every violation from a single validation pass at once, each carrying a JSON-Pointer path to the offending location — a one-violation-per-pass validator costs a full repair round trip per defect, and round trips dominate the cost of validation, not the pass itself. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1] The validator MUST report cycles as data rather than refusing them, per the cycle-legality rule above. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1]

Illustrative only, not a registered wiring — the worked example below uses the conforming component names from `### §0.5`:

```json
{
  "wiring_version": "1",
  "nodes": {
    "chunk":    { "component": "core/chunker-recursive@1.2.0", "config": { "size": 1200 } },
    "embed":    { "component": "core/embedder-bge-m3@3.0.0", "config": { "batch": 32 }, "deps": ["chunk"] },
    "retrieve": { "component": "core/retriever-hybrid@1.0.0", "config": { "topK": 8 }, "deps": ["embed"] }
  },
  "recipe":     { "chunker": "chunk", "embedding": "embed" },
  "harnesses":  [ { "component": "core/harness-crag@1.0.0", "config": { "max_rounds": 3 } } ],
  "provides":   ["retrieve"]
}
```

**Falsifier:** this section is refuted if depth cannot be computed statically from a wiring plus the component registry alone — i.e. if `nodes`, `deps`, and the instance-identity tuple this section freezes do not carry enough information for the validator to derive each node's structural depth without an added self-declared field. That is Falsifier 2 of the selection's falsifier pool, stated here in the wiring format's own terms: if the format needs a self-declared depth field to make §3 computable, this section's identity apparatus is incomplete, not merely §3's rule. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §Falsifiers 2]

## §2 — Node kinds and effects

**Satisfies:** CONTRACT-01

`NodeKind` MUST be represented as a tagged sum — a single-key object whose key selects the kind and whose value is validated by that kind's own schema — stated in a portable vocabulary such as serde external tagging or a pydantic discriminated union, never as a type system that dispatches on a shallow structural check (a shallow check silently picks the first matching branch and every per-kind validation evaporates). [docs-verified] [SELECTION §2]

The tagged sum's members are the primitive part types plus five structural kinds: `fanout`, `join`, `fixpoint`, `subgraph`, and `opaque`. SELECTION.md names the primitive part types only as "~10" without an authoritative list; this section does not invent one — the authoritative enumeration with entry-point signatures is §6's job for the SA-3 migration component and §13's job for socket shapes, and this section cross-references rather than fabricates a list SELECTION.md never froze. [inference] [SELECTION §2]

## §9 — Budget

**Satisfies:** CONTRACT-01

**Budget shape.** A budget MUST be a splittable capability token. Spend — steps, tokens, time — and capacity — the consuming component's context window — MUST be tracked as separate quantities and MUST NOT be conflated into one number; capacity is a property of what a component can hold, spend is a property of what it has used. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §9]

**Fan-out accounting.** Budget accounting under fan-out MUST be **multiplicative by construction** — per `## §2`'s fan-out socket rule, a branch cannot spend what it was not handed, so an `N`-way fan-out's total spend is the sum of `N` independently-budgeted branches, never a shared pool a branch could overrun into a sibling's allocation. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §9] [SELECTION §2]

**Intra-node concurrency — repaired at `V-7`, this plan's Task 2, closing `PARTS.md ## §R N11`.** The fan-out rule above, and `## §1`'s edge-semantics clause, both govern concurrency *between* declared nodes only; neither states what governs concurrency *inside* one node's own execution boundary — a node issuing multiple concurrent internal calls under one already-declared effect over a candidate set the node itself already selected (an opaque ingest lane's own internal parallel calls being the measured case, `PARTS.md ## §R N11`, confirmed live again at the porting-protocol dissection grain by `CATALOG.md ## §D §D-1`, and inherited wholesale by any harness wrapping such a node per `MODEL-RED-TEAM F-13`). **Decision:** intra-node concurrency is **out of this section's budget-shape scope by design** — a node's own internal parallelism (how many concurrent calls it issues, in what order, over what already-selected candidate set) is its private implementation detail, exactly as its internal control flow is; the machine meters and can halt the node only at its declared boundary, via the node's own aggregate spend and wall-clock, tracked exactly as any other node's spend already is under this section's existing rules. This closes the row without minting a new declared field: a node's internal fan-out never itself requires a separate concurrency-cap declaration, because the aggregate-spend metering this section already requires at the node boundary already bounds what that internal parallelism can cost in total, even though it does not schedule or cap the parallelism's shape. This is the same disposition `CATALOG.md ## §D §D-1`'s own porting-protocol repair already adopted for a narrower, adjacent question (whether a node's internal fan-out belongs in a dissection procedure's discard bucket) — this clause generalises that disposition to a full `CONTRACT.md`-level answer for `## §9`'s own budget shape, rather than reopening or contradicting it. ### §13.1 — What a socket type is.

A socket type MUST be defined as the tuple a part declares on each of its input and output ports, built only from vocabulary `## §4` already froze: the accepted or emitted `ItemKind` set (drawn from `## §4`'s open, recipe-declared union — never from a parallel taxonomy this section might otherwise be tempted to invent), the score semantics accepted or emitted (`Score{value, semantics, space_id}` per `## §4`), the `space_id` binding where the port carries embeddings, and the container shape the port carries: a single item, `List[X]`, `List[List[X]]`, `Draft`, or `ContextPackage`, per `## §2`'s `join`/`fanout` socket-shape rules and `## §4`'s `ContextPackage` block shape. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §4] [SELECTION §2]

A socket type MUST be structural, declared, and checked at load — never inferred at runtime by inspecting the shape of data that happens to arrive at a port. A part that ships without a declared socket type on every input and output port is not conformant; the machine has nothing to check the port against until the declaration exists. [inference] [SELECTION §1]

**`Draft`, defined — repaired at `PARTS-06` G1, closing the register's `N5` gap.** `Draft` MUST be defined as `{content: <Answer-shaped payload, text | numeric-array | ranked-items, per §13.4 row 9's typed-answer vocabulary>, producer_ref: <the producing node's own instance identity>, derived_from: [Ref]}` — a candidate, not-yet-finalized output object, distinct from a wiring's genuine terminal `Answer`. Only a node actually named in a wiring's `provides` list emits the run's genuine, non-evidence terminal `Answer`; `§13.4` row 9's "outside the evidence union" note governs that node alone, not every `generator`-kind node in a wiring. Self-consistency's whole-pipeline vote (`map(pipeline, N) → reduce(vote)`) and Speculative RAG's `drafter`/`verifier` selector are the measured cases forcing this definition: both require an `N`-way fan-in over generator-produced candidates, which this row's un-amended text foreclosed by placing every generator's output outside the evidence union without distinguishing a `provides`-named terminal generator from a mid-wiring `drafter`. [inference] [PARTS-06 GS-N24] [PARTS-06 GS-N25]

**A `provides`-named `join`/`grader/filter`, stated because it closes the loop the definition above opens.** A wiring's `provides` list (`§1`) MAY name a `join` or `grader/filter` node whose surviving output is `Draft`-typed; where it does, that surviving `Draft`'s `content` field IS the wiring's terminal `Answer`, read directly, with no separate restating `generator` node required. [inference] [PARTS-06 GS-N24] [PARTS-06 GS-N25]

### §13.2 — Comparing two types across a `deps` edge.

Given two nodes joined by a `deps` edge, the machine MUST run the following ordered, decidable procedure to determine compatibility — a part author can run every step of it by hand without consulting anyone:

Container shape MUST match between the producer's output port and the consumer's input port, or MUST be bridged by an explicit `join` or `fanout` node whose socket shape `## §2` already fixes (`List[List[X]] → List[X]`, `List[Draft] → Draft`, or a fan-out with static or upstream-derived arity); the machine MUST NOT coerce a container-shape mismatch implicitly. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §2]

The producer's emitted `ItemKind` set MUST be a subset of the consumer's accepted `ItemKind` set — the direction stated explicitly so the rule survives paraphrase: a consumer accepting a superset of what arrives can safely ignore the kinds it does not use, while a consumer accepting fewer kinds than what arrives has no way to know which extra kind is safe to silently drop. [inference] [SELECTION §4]

Where a port carries a score, score semantics MUST match between producer and consumer; where they differ, the edge MUST be refused rather than the machine normalising one semantics into another — a cosine value silently treated as a BM25 value is exactly the failure `Score{value, semantics, space_id}` exists to prevent. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §4]

**Fan-in with differing arm semantics — repaired at `PARTS-04` D6.** The refusal rule above governs a single producer-to-consumer edge; a `join` node fed by two or more arms whose emitted score semantics differ is a distinct case this section did not originally distinguish, and applying the rule's literal consequence there would refuse every rank-fusion fan-in on the record — including LightRAG's own arm interleaves (`join-entities`/`join-relations`/`join-chunks`, whose two arms are ordered by incomparable criteria: `sorted(key=(rank, weight))` at `operate.py:5887-5889` versus vector-similarity order at `:6100`, with no score read anywhere in the merge block) and HippoRAG 2's PPR-plus-dense case. **A `join` node whose arms differ in emitted score semantics MUST declare a fusion policy** — reciprocal-rank fusion, a declared weighted combination, or round-robin-by-position — and the join's own emitted score semantics MUST be either the declared policy's own semantics, or, where the policy is a positional interleave with no score read, the declared semantics `rank_position`. The general refusal rule fires only where **no** fusion policy is declared at such a fan-in — a `join` merging differing-semantics arms with neither a declared policy nor a declared `rank_position` emission is refused exactly as the general rule already states, unchanged. This does not relax the general rule at an ordinary producer-consumer edge; it names the fan-in case's own conformant declaration, which the general rule's text did not anticipate. [docs-verified] [PARTS-04 D6]

Where a port carries embeddings, `space_id` MUST match between the producer's declared write space and the consumer's declared read space; `reads_space` and `writes_space` declarations MUST be typechecked at load, per `## §4`'s `EmbeddingSpace` rule. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §4]

An `opaque` producer declares its emitted kinds in its own manifest; the machine MUST check that declaration at wire time exactly as it checks any other node's declared sockets, and MUST additionally audit the declaration at run time via `refs_in`/`refs_resolved` per `## §4` — a manifest is a self-report, and an opaque node's self-report earns no exemption from the audit every node's evidence flow is already subject to. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §4] [SELECTION §8]

The kind registry MUST remain open: a recipe MAY declare a further `ItemKind` beyond the seven `## §4` enumerates, and a consumer MUST NOT accept a kind it did not declare accepting. Undeclared is denied here exactly as `## §2`'s deny-by-default clause denies it everywhere else in this contract. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §2] [SELECTION Lineup-wide 2]

Per-kind schemas MUST be selected by an explicit discriminator field, never by a shallow structural match that picks the first branch whose shape happens to fit. This is a measured trap: a type system that dispatches on a shallow structural check silently picks the first matching branch, and every per-kind validation behind the branches it never reaches evaporates. [code-verified] [SELECTION §2]

On an incompatible edge, the machine MUST return a validator violation carrying a JSON-Pointer path to the offending socket, reported alongside every other violation from the same validation pass at once, per `## §1`'s validator-behaviour clause — a socket-type mismatch is not a special case that earns its own one-violation-per-pass round trip. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1]

Container shapes match (`List[X]` on both sides), the emitted kind (`text_chunk`) is a subset of the accepted kind, and no score exists on the producing side to conflict with the consumer's declared score semantics. The edge validates. [inference] [new-synthesis]

## §15 — Where a new storage need lands

**Satisfies:** CONTRACT-09

### §15.1 — The procedure.

A new storage need MUST be resolved by trying four branches in a fixed order; the first branch whose test passes is where the need lands, and no later branch is consulted once one passes. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**Branch 1 — sub-capability on an existing store type.** Test: does the need reduce to a capability declaration one of the five store types (KV, vector, graph, blob, lexical — `## §14.2`'s five real store backends, repaired at `V-2` to correct an earlier six-way count tangle `MODEL-RED-TEAM.md F-10` found; `recipe`-level sub-capabilities are checked separately, per `## §14.2`'s own relocated clause) already carries or could carry as a sub-capability per `## §14.2`, without changing the data shape that store type holds? A part that needs graph-shaped pattern queries over its existing graph store, exhaustive vector scoring, or a run-scoped KV write lands here without touching this section again. [docs-verified] [GAP-SWEEP §3.2]

**Branch 2 — opaque blob artifact plus sidecar manifest.** Test: can the need be served by treating the storage as content-addressed bytes the machine holds and does not parse — a blob artifact with a sidecar provenance manifest per `## §7` — with any internal structure interpreted entirely by the part that owns it? [docs-verified] [SELECTION §7]

**Branch 3 — `self_storage`.** Test: is the need private to one producing instance, never read by any node but its own producer, at any depth? [docs-verified] [SELECTION §3]

**Branch 4 — new store kind.** Test: does serving the need require the machine to gain a sixth native store type — a new primitive the validator, the artifact registry, and every capability table in `## §14` must be extended to recognize? A `yes` here is a contract-level change, not a decision a wiring author or part can make unilaterally at authoring time, exactly as creating a namespace is a contract-level change at `## §0`. [inference] [CONTEXT D-02]

Because each branch has a decidable test and the branches are tried in fixed order, the procedure terminates on every real input: either branches 1–3 place the need before branch 4 is reached, or all three fail and branch 4's own test then decides — cleanly, either as a genuine new-store-kind case, or, as `### §15.2` shows for one half of the worked case, as a decisive negative that redirects the need out of the storage-need ladder entirely rather than leaving it undecided. [inference] [new-synthesis]

### §15.2 — The worked case: relational/SQL.

The case splits in two before either half is run through the procedure, exactly as REQUIREMENTS.md's owner-raised priority #2 frames it: **machine-store SQL** — the machine retains relational-shaped data as part of its own durable storage — and **external system-of-record SQL** — a database the machine queries but does not own, reached through a served tool for evidence the machine consumes without taking ownership of it. These are different needs, carried through all four branches separately below, and they land differently. [docs-verified] [new-synthesis]

**Machine-store SQL, branch by branch.**

Branch 1: the nearest existing analog MUST be weighed explicitly, not skipped — the graph store's `formal-query-language` sub-capability (`## §14.2`), which already grants schema-validated query-string execution (SPARQL/Cypher-shaped) as a third mode beside pointwise ops and bulk-export. It buys a structured, schema-checked *query surface*; it does not buy a different *data shape*. Genuine relational modeling — normalized tables joined on foreign keys, transactional multi-row consistency — is not expressible as graph traversal regardless of the query language accepted at the boundary; extending `formal-query-language` to parse SQL syntax would change what strings the store accepts, not what shape of data it holds. **Branch 1 fails**: the mismatch is in data shape, not query surface, and no sub-capability closes a data-shape gap. [inference] [GAP-SWEEP §3.2]

Branch 2: a relational database a part maintains for itself — whether it embeds its own engine (SQLite, DuckDB) or computes a result set via a served relational tool such as `postgres-mcp` — reduces, once the part is done with it, to bytes the machine can hold: a content-addressed blob artifact with a sidecar manifest recording its schema and provenance, exactly as `## §7` already requires for any blob. **Branch 2 passes**: declared capabilities are `effects[] ⊇ {writes_artifact}`, `storage: machine`, and a sidecar manifest naming the blob's internal schema — no new store kind, no new node kind, just an ordinary blob write. **`postgres-mcp` exemplifies this half**: the part reaching it treats the live relational computation as the means, and the durable artifact this section decides about is the retained result, not the transport that produced it — the transport question (how a live relational engine is reached) belongs to `## §17`, not to this section's branch. [inference] [new-synthesis]

Because Branch 2 passes, branches 3 and 4 are not reached for this half.

**External system-of-record SQL, branch by branch.**

Branch 1: the test cannot even be posed on its own terms — a sub-capability extends a store type the machine itself owns and holds data in, and an external system-of-record is, by definition, not data the machine owns or holds. **Branch 1 fails structurally**, before any particular sub-capability (including `formal-query-language`, weighed above) is relevant to it. [inference] [new-synthesis]

Branch 2: the data is not static, machine-held bytes; it is a live, independently-administered system the machine reaches through calls at query time and never copies into its own artifact store. There is nothing to content-address. **Branch 2 fails**. [inference] [new-synthesis]

Branch 3: `self_storage` scopes what a producing instance privately *writes and retains*; a system-of-record the machine only *reads*, and does not administer, is not written by the machine at all — this is not a "who may read what the machine wrote" question in the first place. **Branch 3 fails**, for the same structural reason as branches 1 and 2. [inference] [new-synthesis]

Branch 4: does serving this need require the machine to gain a sixth native store type? **No** — and this is the decisive answer for this half, not merely a fourth failure. The correct admission path for a foreign, non-machine-owned queryable system is not to extend the machine's own store-type vocabulary at all; it is to admit the query tool as a node under `## §8`'s admission conditions, restated MCP-specifically at `## §17`. **`supabase-mcp` exemplifies this half**: a Supabase-backed project's own database, administered independently of this machine, queried live for evidence the machine consumes without ever taking ownership of it as one of its own artifacts. Branch 4's test redirects rather than passes: **external system-of-record SQL is not a storage need this ladder decides; it is a node-admission need**, decided exactly where `## §17`'s key link to this section says it should be. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**The `sql_result_set` `ItemKind`.** Its presence in `## §4`'s union settles that SQL-shaped evidence — rows and columns — MAY cross a socket boundary as typed, machine-checkable evidence exactly like any other kind. It does **not** settle where the producing store lives: a `sql_result_set` item may be emitted by a part reading its own blob-persisted database (Branch 2), by a foreign engine reached through an opaque/`external-tool` node (`## §8`/`## §17`), or, hypothetically, by a machine-native relational store this section has just ruled unnecessary (Branch 4). The kind's membership in the union is an evidence-typing fact, orthogonal to the storage-need decision this section makes. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §4]

**Weighing the gap-sweep finding.** "No sixth store type is needed by anything in scope" is the gap sweep's finding over 45 surveyed systems — the sweep's own words call it "the sweep's single most reassuring finding." [docs-verified] [GAP-SWEEP §3.2] It MUST be treated as evidence bearing on Branch 4, not as the answer to this case: the 45 systems surveyed are RAG-modality paradigms (video, audio, KAG, debate, DSI, ROME/MEMIT, RAFT, RL retrieval, geospatial, recommendation, time-series, and others), not database-access tools, and `postgres-mcp`/`supabase-mcp` were **not** inside that survey — they are the two concretes this contract itself names for the CONTRACT-09 worked case, not entries the gap sweep already classified. [docs-verified] [GAP-SWEEP §3.2] That the four-branch procedure, run independently on both halves of this worked case, also lands on "no new store kind" is corroborating, not derivative — it is this document's own conclusion, reached by walking the procedure the gap sweep's finding does not itself walk. [inference] [new-synthesis]

### §15.3 — Verdict.

**Verdict:** Machine-store SQL lands on **Branch 2** — opaque blob artifact plus sidecar manifest. Declared capabilities: `effects[] ⊇ {writes_artifact}`, `storage: machine`, sidecar manifest naming the retained relational data's schema. No new store kind follows from this half of the case. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**Verdict:** External system-of-record SQL lands on none of the four storage-need branches — Branch 4's test resolves it as a node-admission need rather than a storage need, admitted under `## §8`'s conditions, restated MCP-specifically at `## §17`. Declared capabilities are the ones `## §17` names for a served relational query tool: a wall-clock ceiling, `storage: self-contained`, a declared accepted/emitted `ItemKind` set including `sql_result_set`, and exclusion from the default selector while effective depth stays `opaque`. [inference] [new-synthesis]

The two halves land differently, and that is the useful result of running the procedure rather than assuming it: identical query language (SQL) and identical evidence typing (`sql_result_set`) do not imply identical storage-need placement — ownership of the data is what the branches actually test, not the query language used to reach it. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**What would change either verdict.** The machine-store verdict would move off Branch 2 if a surveyed modality demonstrated an actual need for the machine to execute relational joins natively across its own artifact registry as a first-class machine service, rather than merely retaining relationally-produced bytes — that is a Branch 4 case this worked example did not find. The external-half verdict would move out of node-admission entirely if a concrete case blurred the ownership line this section relies on — a system genuinely co-administered by this machine and an external party — at which point the case would need re-splitting rather than a different branch under the current split. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**Falsifier:** the procedure is refuted if a storage need arising in Phases 3–4 runs all four branches without any test passing, or if two authors running it on the same need reach different branches. [inference] [new-synthesis]

## §16 — Artifact discovery and pinning

**Satisfies:** CONTRACT-10

This section is new synthesis over frozen material: `## §7` defines what the artifact registry stores, but no prior document specifies the lookup a wiring author performs against it. Clauses below carry `[new-synthesis]` as their trace tag unless they restate `## §3` or `## §7` directly, in which case they carry those sections' trace tags instead. [inference] [new-synthesis]

### §16.1 — The discovery operation.

**When it runs.** The discovery lookup is admissible at exactly two times: **authoring time**, when a wiring author asks what artifacts exist and what they may read before writing a wiring, and **load time**, when the machine resolves and freezes the artifact set a run will actually read. It MUST NOT run during a run: `## §11` already states that artifact-plane values are resolved once at load and passed into the running component as data, and that a component MUST NOT query the registry during a run because **a control channel is a read** — the same rule that forbids reading the ledger or scoreboard mid-run forbids querying the artifact registry mid-run for the identical reason. [docs-verified] [SELECTION Conditions 2]

**The query shape.** An author MAY query the artifact registry by: recipe compatibility, tested via SA-1 recipe identity and SA-2 sub-recipe stamps (chunker, extraction, embedding); corpus id; `space_id`; producing instance; and scope. These are exactly the fields `## §7`'s artifact registry already records — namespace (the artifact's scope), sub-recipe stamps, corpus, `space_id`, and producing instance — so the query shape is a filter over what the registry already carries, not a new field the registry must be extended to hold. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §7] A query MUST NOT be able to return an artifact the caller may not read: the scope filter — `shared` visible to any wiring, `quarantined` visible only under an explicit pin naming the caller, `self_storage` visible only to its own producing instance — is applied by the registry itself as part of executing the query, never left to the caller to apply after the fact. [inference] [SELECTION §3]

**The result shape.** A discovery result MUST carry: the artifact's scope (`shared`/`quarantined`/`self_storage`), its SA-2 sub-recipe stamps, its `space_id` where applicable, its producing instance, its retention tier (`runnable`/`readable`/`tombstoned`, per `## §7`), and whether it is readable directly or only under a pin. A result that omitted the scope or the pin-required flag would not let an author answer the question this operation exists to answer — whether their wiring may read it — from the result alone. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**The reuse question.** This operation is what makes D-08's rebuild-avoidance axis decidable rather than aspirational: recipe-hash identity lets the machine prove an index is unchanged between two recipe versions, and the discovery result — carrying the sub-recipe stamps a wiring's own recipe can be compared against — is the input `## §6`'s reindex planner needs to classify an artifact `reuse` rather than `re-embed`/`re-extract`/`rebuild`. Without a discovery operation returning sub-recipe stamps in a form the planner can compare, `reuse` is a claim with nothing to check it against; with it, `reuse` is a decidable classification. [docs-verified] [CONTEXT D-08] [SELECTION §6]

### §16.2 — The pin.

**What a pin is.** A pin is wiring data: an explicit, author-written reference naming the artifact and the instance that produced it. A pin MUST NOT be implicit or glob-matched — a wiring author writes down exactly which artifact, produced by exactly which instance, their wiring depends on, consistent with `## §3`'s single-provenance rule for `quarantined` artifacts. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §3]

**Validation.** A pin MUST be validated at load, against the load-frozen registry snapshot the run's other artifact-plane values are also resolved from (`## §11`) — never validated only at authoring time and trusted thereafter, since the artifact the pin names may have changed state between when the wiring was written and when it runs. A pin is invalid if: the named artifact has been garbage-collected; the artifact is tombstoned; the artifact was produced by a different instance than the one the pin names; or the reader's wiring is not entitled to it. An invalid pin MUST fail the run closed — the run does not start, or the node depending on the pin does not admit — rather than degrading to reading nothing or reading a substitute; a silent degrade-to-empty would let a mistaken pin pass unnoticed exactly where `## §4`'s `refs_resolved < refs_in` refusal already refuses the analogous silent-degrade failure for evidence refs. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**The taint consequence.** A run that pins a `quarantined` artifact MUST compute its own effective depth as if it depended on that artifact's opaque producer, exactly as `## §3` already states: the pinning run's own outputs go to `quarantined` or `self_storage` and MUST NOT go to `shared`. [code-verified] [spike 005] This section restates the rule here because the pin is where the operation actually happens — an author reading `## §3` alone would know the rule exists but not where in the wiring lifecycle it is enforced. The same split tagging `## §3` uses applies here unchanged: the within-run half of this consequence is `[code-verified]`, measured directly by `taint.py`; the cross-run half — that a pin in one run constrains that reader's own shared writes at all — is `[inference]` and explicitly **unmeasured**, since `taint.py` models within-wiring dependency taint only, never a reader in a separate run pinning another run's artifact. [code-verified] [inference] [spike 005] The falsifier experiment that would justify loosening this rule is the same one `## §3` names: a cross-run laundering test, structured like spike 005's within-run test, showing that a run pinning a `quarantined` artifact and writing to `shared` produces no artifact indistinguishable from a legitimately `stage`-produced one. Until that experiment runs, the rule stays strict — loosening later with evidence is safe, tightening it later is not. [inference] [CONTEXT D-06]

**Refcounting.** Pinning an artifact MUST increment a reference count on the artifact and on the instance that produced it. GC MUST NOT collect a pinned artifact, and MUST NOT collect the producing instance while any of its artifacts remain pinned. Unpinning MUST decrement the reference count; the artifact becomes collectible only when its reference count reaches zero **and** the producing instance is no longer reachable, per `## §7`'s recipe-version reachability rule — a refcount reaching zero is necessary but not sufficient, since the producing instance may still be reachable through an unrelated author-declared or runtime-minted node. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §7] A pin held by a run that halts or completes only partially is not released merely because the run did not finish: `## §9` already states that a halted or partial run MUST be traced, scored, and tier-labeled exactly as a completed run is, never discarded, and the same first-class treatment extends to any pin that halted run holds — the pin's refcount contribution persists until the run's own record is explicitly retired, not silently dropped because the run never reached its `provides` nodes. [inference] [SELECTION §9]

**Falsifier:** the operation is refuted if a wiring author in Phases 3–4 cannot determine from the discovery result alone whether their wiring may read an artifact, or if the refcount rule permits a pinned artifact to be collected. [inference] [new-synthesis]

## §17 — Foreign-part adapter contract

**Satisfies:** CONTRACT-11

This section restates `## §8`'s generic opaque-node admission conditions MCP-specifically; it MUST NOT contradict them. Where this section and `## §8` appear to differ, `## §8` governs and this section is in error — this section elaborates the generic contract for one hosting shape, it does not amend it. [inference] [SELECTION §8]

**Node kind choice.** An MCP-served engine MUST be admitted with the primitive part type `external-tool` (`## §13.4` row 10) and MUST be hosted via the `opaque` structural kind (`## §2`) — the two are the same admission, not alternative choices: `external-tool` states *what* the node is (a foreign engine's served tool surface), `opaque` states *how* the machine hosts it, because an MCP server's internals are not machine-visible by construction, which is exactly the condition `## §8` gates its full eleven-condition admission list on. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**Transport.** The adapter owns process lifecycle (starting, monitoring, and terminating the MCP server process), the RPC channel (the MCP protocol's request/response framing), and health — as a fixed cost the machine pays once per engine *shape*, not a broker rewritten per engine. This framing is adopted lineup-wide: the foreign-part adapter is priced as a fixed cost, not a variant discriminator — process lifecycle, RPC, health, corpus-feed handoff, and evidence normalization exist in any design that hosts a foreign engine. [docs-verified] [SELECTION Lineup-wide 11] **Falsifier 6** is what would refute this framing: implement the adapter for two differently-shaped engines (`codebase-memory-mcp`: no LLM, own storage; a LightRAG server: LLM-heavy, injectable endpoint) — if each needs its own broker rewritten rather than sharing this section's admission machinery, the fixed-cost claim is an arithmetic error, not a design property. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §Falsifiers 6]

**Corpus-feed handoff.** The corpus feed delivered to an MCP-served node MUST be at a declared `feed_tier`, per `## §8` condition 6; a chunk-tier feed MUST be refused where the receiving recipe is embedder-coupled, per `## §2`'s embedder-coupling refusal, regardless of which side of the MCP boundary the feed crosses from. [code-verified] [SELECTION §2] [SELECTION §8]

**Declared effects.** An MCP-served part MUST declare its effects from `## §2`'s `effects[]` vocabulary and its capabilities from `## §14`'s tables, exactly as any other node must; undeclared is denied. The machine MUST refuse at wire time any wiring whose parts' declarations are mutually unsatisfiable — a wiring that pairs a part requiring a capability with a hosting context that cannot supply it fails at wire time, not at run time. The verified precedent, worked through rather than merely cited: `codebase-memory-mcp` requires *zero* machine primitives and has no LLM anywhere; an LLM-requiring transformer wired into a `codebase-memory-mcp`-hosted part MUST be refused at wire time, because the two parts' declarations — one declaring no `calls_llm` capability anywhere in its closure, one requiring it — are mutually unsatisfiable the instant the validator checks them against each other, not merely under-declared individually. **Evidence normalization.** Everything a foreign engine returns MUST be normalized to `## §4`'s `ItemKind` union at the adapter boundary — the point where foreign output becomes machine evidence. A kind with no existing union member MUST be declared by the recipe under `## §4`'s open-registry rule rather than passed through untyped; an untyped pass-through is exactly the "kind MUST NOT be represented as a bare scalar field" failure `## §4` already forbids, now located at the specific boundary where it is most likely to be reintroduced. This restates `## §13.2`'s requirement that a manifest-declared emitted-kind set is checked at wire time and additionally audited at run time via `refs_in`/`refs_resolved` — an opaque node's self-report earns no exemption from that audit, and an MCP-served node is exactly the opaque-node case `## §13.2` already names. **Health and lifecycle.** The adapter owns the health check that determines whether the MCP server process is live and serving. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §3] [SELECTION §9]

### §18.4 — Selecting a modality or a harness.

- **A stable alias** — the promoted alias `## §6`'s mutation-promotion clause mints at the atomic alias repoint, the machine's stable public handle for whatever is currently active behind it. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §6]
- **A declared capability or capability set** — any capability `## §14`'s tables enumerate, letting a consumer ask for "whatever satisfies this capability" without naming which component supplies it. [inference] [new-synthesis]
- **A harness by name**, drawn from the ordered `harnesses` array `## §1` already freezes. [docs-verified] [SELECTION §1]
- **The default selector** — no selector at all, deferring to the machine's own default. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**Unsatisfiable selector.** If no fitted modality satisfies the selector a consumer names, the machine MUST return an explicit refusal naming what was missing, never a silent fallback to the default — a silent fallback would answer a query from a modality the consumer did not select, exactly the attribution failure `## §18.3`'s invariance rule and this closed-selector rule together exist to prevent. ### §18.5 — How the tool surface may grow.

**The tool surface MAY grow per part and MUST NOT grow per modality.** A new part kind that exposes a genuinely new operation earns a new MCP/REST tool; a second modality that answers an operation an existing tool already exposes MUST add no tool. [inference] [new-synthesis]

[inference] [new-synthesis]

**One rule, two sides.** A per-modality tool would make modality substitution visible at the seam — a consumer calling a modality-named tool has, by construction, named the modality — which `## §18.3`'s invariance clause already forbids. The surface-growth rule and the invariance rule are therefore one rule seen from two sides: invariance forbids a modality-specific field in what the seam returns; the growth rule forbids a modality-specific tool in what the seam exposes to be called. [inference] [new-synthesis]

**Falsifier:** the seam is refuted if any of the three Phase 3 stress modalities cannot be reached through this seam without a modality-specific field or a modality-specific tool, or if a consumer needs a wiring name — rather than an alias, a capability, or a harness name — to select what it wants. ## §19 — Node granularity

**Satisfies:** PARTS-04

Node granularity — which operations become their own node versus which fuse into a neighbour — is decided by what can be varied and measured, never by what the ported source happened to factor into a function. This section fills the gap `03-WIRING-FORM-FINDINGS.md` finding 2 named: no prior clause fixed node granularity, so the same modality yielded opposite verdicts from the same clauses. ### §19.1 — Port completeness (a diagnostic with mandatory disposition).

Every value a candidate node exchanges with another node in the wiring MUST be expressible as a `## §13.1` socket type on one of its declared ports. Where two operations exchange anything no port carries — a shared mutable structure, a store handle threaded past the wiring, a sibling's intermediate result consumed positionally, or a parameter one operation derives from how much another produced — the author MUST either lift the coupling into a declared `deps` edge or record the operations as one fused node with a `PARTS.md ## §R` row naming the coupling. A node set published with an unlifted, unrecorded coupling is non-conformant. This diagnostic cannot itself *refuse* a wiring, because `## §13.2` keeps the `ItemKind` registry open — any crossing value can be made port-expressible by declaring a kind — so its content is a mandatory disposition (lift or fuse-and-register), not a pass/fail gate. [docs-verified] [03-LIGHTRAG-DECOMPOSITION §The granularity ruling] [CONTEXT D-09]

### §19.2 — Store-mediated self-coupling.

A node's cache partition key MUST be derivable from its `(name@version, config_hash, resolved_dependency_ids)` tuple plus its declared input values, and MUST NOT include any field outside them. A determinism re-run under `## §5` MUST be performed with the node's cache bypassed — a cache-served re-run would match trivially and make the re-run vacuous. [docs-verified] [03-LIGHTRAG-DECOMPOSITION §The granularity ruling] [CONTEXT D-09]

### §19.3 — Budget closure.

Every budget a node spends MUST be either its own allocation or a declared apportionment at the node holding the allowance. That apportioning node — whether or not it is a `join` — MUST declare its apportionment policy; the policy MUST be part of **its** `config_hash` under `## §1`; and a run record MUST stamp each contributing branch's realised share. `## §5` MUST refuse a comparison whose arms' realised shares differ beyond the declared mutation's scope; that refusal is `## §5`'s refusal list's **eleventh** condition, landed in place — together with this clause's own restatement at `## §9` — by `PARTS-04`'s D4 repair. [docs-verified] [03-LIGHTRAG-DECOMPOSITION §The granularity ruling] [CONTEXT D-10] [PARTS-01 D4]

### §19.4 — Variance bearing (a cut requirement) — repaired at R1, R6, V-5, V-6.

An operation MUST be cut as its own node when either: (a) the ported source exposes a **knob** at that boundary, or (c) fusing the operation into its consumer would force that consumer to declare an `effects[]` member it does not otherwise exercise. An operation for which neither holds MUST NOT be cut. **A knob is a substitution or bypass point at that boundary** — a place where the operation can be replaced by a different implementation or skipped entirely — excluding a swappable inner algorithm whose alternatives share one socket and one effects union, which is config, not a cut. **A numeric parameter of an operation's internal policy is config, not a knob**, and does not by itself force a cut. **Worked negative case, named so the boundary is checkable rather than merely stated:** a value derived at the call site from how much another operation produced — for example a residual token budget computed as `total budget − fixed overheads − a sibling's realised spend` — is not a knob, because it is not independently settable; it is `## §19.1`'s derived-parameter case, not a cut boundary, and MUST NOT be read as licensing a standalone node under limb (a). **Precedence where the knob sentence and the swappable-inner-algorithm exclusion both capture one site — repaired at R6.** The exclusion clause narrows what counts as config; it does not reopen a genuine bypass or substitution point the knob sentence's first clause already captures. Where a boundary IS a genuine bypass/substitution point — a caller-visible place where the operation can be skipped entirely or replaced by a different implementation, as distinct from an internal choice among sub-algorithm variants the operation itself always runs — **the knob sentence controls**, and the boundary MUST be cut, even where its alternatives converge to one socket and one effects union. The exclusion clause governs only a site with no such external skip/substitute point at all: an unconditionally-invoked operation where a config value merely selects which inner algorithm runs (`## §19.4`'s own worked case is `kg_chunk_pick_method`: the chunk-selection call is made on every path: only which downstream algorithm it dispatches to varies, and no external caller can skip or replace the call itself — the exclusion clause governs there, and this precedence rule leaves that outcome undisturbed). This antecedent is narrow by design: a site with no bypass/substitution point at all is untouched by this precedence rule and remains governed by the exclusion clause on its own terms. [docs-verified] [03-GRANULARITY-EXPERIMENT §Ambiguity harvest R1] [PARTS-04 R1] [03-GRANULARITY-BLIND §New defects] [PARTS-04 R6]

**Limb (c)'s evaluation scope — repaired at `V-5`, this plan's Task 2, closing `PARTS.md ## §R N7`.** Limb (c) is evaluated **over the union of the candidate operation's own call sites within the consumer node, checked against the effects the consumer's *other*, non-candidate operations already independently require** — never per individual call site of the candidate operation alone, and never by treating the candidate operation itself as its own reference point. Concretely: fusing candidate operation `X` into consumer node `C` forces a cut under limb (c) if and only if the `effects[]` member `X` would contribute is **not** already required by at least one of `C`'s other, already-retained operations — i.e., fusion would add a genuinely new member to `C`'s declared `effects[]` set that `C` would not otherwise need to declare. Where every one of `X`'s own exercising paths is itself the operation under evaluation and `C` has no other retained operation to check against, the test resolves as **not forced**: an operation with zero independent reference points inside its consumer contributes no effect `C` does not already exhibit through `X` itself, and limb (c) does not license a cut on the strength of an operation checking only against its own presence. This closes the circularity `PARTS.md ## §R N7` named: the ambiguity was not knowing whether the test ran per-site or over a union, and whether a candidate operation could be its own forcing reference; both are now fixed, with the reference set drawn from the consumer's *other* operations rather than from the candidate itself. [inference] [MODEL-RED-TEAM F-12] [PARTS §R N7]

**Fusion-neighbour absorption — repaired at `V-6`, this plan's Task 2, closing `PARTS.md ## §R N8`.** Where this section forbids a cut at a boundary (an operation MUST NOT be its own node) and more than one adjacent neighbour is available to absorb the un-cut operation, the operation MUST be absorbed by whichever neighbour satisfies, in order, the first of these two tests that applies: **(1) guard-scope match** — the neighbour whose own `## §19.8` data-guard scope already covers the operation's condition, where such a neighbour exists, since the operation is then already inside that neighbour's own conditional-execution boundary and absorbing it there introduces no new guard; **(2) tightest single dataflow dependency** — where no neighbour's guard scope matches (including where no neighbour declares a guard at all), the operation MUST be absorbed by the neighbour it feeds most directly with no intervening branch point, i.e. the one neighbour whose input the un-cut operation's output reaches without passing through a `fanout` or `join` structural kind first. This is the guard-scope-match heuristic `PARTS.md ## §R N8` records two independent blind authors already converging on by inference; it is now this section's own stated rule rather than an unstated precedent a third porter would have to reconstruct. [inference] [MODEL-RED-TEAM F-05] [MODEL-RED-TEAM F-12] [PARTS §R N8]

### §19.5 — Attribution closure (a cut requirement).

Where two nodes can emit the same item, the fan-in merging them MUST emit that item's provenance as **set-valued** — the set of contributing node positions — **machine-stamped** at the socket boundary. A fan-in that credits a multiply-produced item to one arm by outer-list order is not attribution-closed and MUST NOT be published as separating those arms. [docs-verified] [03-LIGHTRAG-DECOMPOSITION §The granularity ruling] [CONTEXT D-09]

Where a node's internals are not machine-visible, granularity is decided over its declared ports, not its operations: two invocations MUST be separate ports where they differ in emitted `ItemKind` set, score semantics, or declared effects, and MUST be one port otherwise. `## §19.1` is not desk-checkable for such a node and is discharged by `## §13.2`'s run-time `refs_in`/`refs_resolved` audit instead. ### §19.8 — Conditional execution.

A node MAY be data-guarded provided the wiring declares the guard, the guard's evaluating node, and the socket value emitted when the guard does not fire; the declaration enters the guarded node's `config_hash` under `## §1`, so two wirings differing only in a guard are two identities rather than one identity carrying two behaviours, and the run record stamps whether the guard fired. `## §5` MUST refuse a comparison whose arms differ in which guards fired — the tenth condition on that section's refusal list, per the amendment there. This declaration requirement — not a blanket prohibition on data-guarded nodes — is what gives `## §14.3`'s mandatory client-capability degradation paths a contract home, since a degradation path is conditional execution on run-time data by definition; `## §14.3`'s four flags are this rule's worked precedent, not an exception carved out from it. [docs-verified] [03-LIGHTRAG-DECOMPOSITION §The granularity ruling] [CONTEXT D-10]

**Observability, using the mechanism `## §18.2` already provides.** A guard's firing MUST be observable per query, not merely stamped on an internal run record no consumer can read: because a declared guard **is** a degradation path under this section's own equivalence with `## §14.3`, a guard's firing is exactly the event `## §18.2`'s response envelope already surfaces — "any degradation path a manifest declared and the run actually took" — and this section mints no new envelope field to carry it. The run-record stamp this section already requires is what populates that existing envelope field; a consumer comparing arms across a guarded boundary can therefore identify, per query, which queries the guard fired on, and partition or report them separately rather than losing them to an unattributed average. [inference] [PARTS-01 D10]

**The guard-granularity rule — answers `ANATOMY.md ## §F` DR-07.** A guard's declaration MUST assign it exactly one of two granularities: **per-query**, evaluated fresh against that query's own runtime data, or **per-instance**, fixed at `config_hash` resolution and held for the resolved instance's whole lifetime. The two are not interchangeable and neither subsumes the other: a per-instance guard's firing is knowable at wire time from the resolved config alone — an ordinary `config_hash` mismatch is what a divergence there already is, under `## §1` — while a per-query guard's firing is knowable only from the run record after the query executes, which is exactly what `## §5`'s new tenth refusal condition reads. Selection is therefore routed per-query for a guard declared at that granularity and configured per-session for a guard declared at the other — this contract does not pick one answer for every guard; the wiring's own declaration does. [docs-verified] [PARTS-01 D10]

**The equal-node-set rule.** Where two arms of one base wiring resolve, under some or all runtime data, to exactly equal node sets, a wiring MUST state which of exactly three outcomes applies: **(1) one arm with a declared guard** — the arm the fallback produces is not a second independent arm at all, but a data-guarded variant of the arm it converges with, declared under this section's vocabulary; **(2) collision as one identity** — the two arms are the same wiring under `## §1`'s identity tuple and MUST be registered as one arm, never two; or **(3) distinct arms by another declared field** — the two arms keep separate identities despite the equal node set, distinguished by a field this section does not itself name (a differing `config_hash` on an otherwise-identical node, for instance). A wiring silent on which of the three applies is non-conformant under this rule, exactly as an unlifted `## §19.1` coupling is non-conformant under that one. [inference] [PARTS-01 D10]

This settles the vocabulary `## §19.8` has carried forward since it was minted: the declaration requirement, the guard-granularity rule and the equal-node-set rule now stand in place, in this section, rather than promised for a later plan. [inference] [PARTS-01 D10]

### §19.9 — Effects under a selectable strategy — repaired at R3.

A node's declared `effects[]` MUST be the union over every reachable strategy, including strategies reached only by a runtime fallback. **The embedding-client effects member.** `## §2`'s `effects[]` vocabulary gains `calls_embedding`, parallel to `calls_llm` and `calls_rerank` — a node whose reachable strategy calls the embedding client MUST declare it, exactly as any other reachable-strategy effect this clause already governs; `## §14.1` mirrors this member in its own list. **The fallback ruling.** A store-internal embed reached only by a runtime fallback IS a reachable strategy under this clause, not an exempt corner case: a node that can reach the embedding client only through such a fallback still MUST declare `calls_embedding`, because the effects union this clause requires is computed over every reachable strategy, not only the strategy a given configuration exercises by default. [docs-verified] [03-GRANULARITY-EXPERIMENT §Ambiguity harvest R3] [PARTS-04 R3]

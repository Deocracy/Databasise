# Lane 10 findings: Evaluating harnesses and turning harness signals into training rewards

Lane question: which harness signals should become MelodyScribe's training rewards, and how is a harness compared against another harness in theory before a model is trained?

Short answer: the literature compares harnesses empirically and post-hoc, never analytically before training (see Refutations R4). The reward signals with the strongest evidence are: downstream answer exact-match as the primary outcome reward; retrieval-invocation and format rewards as staged shaping; memory-operation choice (ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP) rewarded by downstream QA; rule-based verifiable checks (validator pass, AST match) instead of learned reward models; and abstention/consistency metrics (pass^k, irrelevance detection) as harness-score components. Latency is reported as capacity under a tail-latency SLO, not raw tokens/s.

## (a) Admitted items

**1. LoCoMo — very-long-conversation memory benchmark (arXiv:2402.17753; ACL 2024; Maharana, Lee, Tulyakov, Bansal, Barbieri, Fang).**
What it is: 50 very-long dialogues (~300 turns, ~9K tokens average, up to 35 sessions) generated by LLM agents grounded on personas and event graphs, human-verified, with QA, event-summarization and multimodal-generation tasks.
Take from it: the closest public harness-comparison rig for MelodyScribe's recall side: it separates single-hop, multi-hop, temporal, open-domain and adversarial questions, so a harness can be scored per capability rather than by one QA number. Memory-R1's later gains are measured on exactly these splits.
Be careful: the ACL camera-ready version reports ~600 turns/16K tokens per dialogue while the arXiv v1 reports ~300 turns/9K tokens; cite the version used. RAG and long-context baselines both substantially lag humans, so beating a weak baseline here proves little.

**2. LongMemEval — five-ability memory benchmark plus a design framework (arXiv:2410.10813; ICLR 2025; Wu, Wang, Yu, Zhang, Chang, Yu; code https://github.com/xiaowu0162/LongMemEval).**
What it is: 500 curated questions over scalable histories covering information extraction, multi-session reasoning, knowledge updates, temporal reasoning and abstention, with a unified indexing-retrieval-reading decomposition of any memory harness.
Take from it: three things. First, the five abilities (especially abstention and knowledge updates) are the right scorecard for MelodyScribe recall, and commercial assistants drop ~30% accuracy moving to sustained interaction. Second, the ablations give harness settings with numbers: round-granularity values beat sessions, fact-augmented keys help indexing, time-aware expansion helps temporal questions. Third, the reading stage matters as much as retrieval: a suboptimal reader loses up to 10 absolute points even under oracle retrieval, and JSON formatting with Chain-of-Note reading is the recommended default.
Be careful: histories are synthetic and the commercial-system comparisons used 10x-shortened histories with human operators, so those gaps are directional, not exact.

**3. BRIGHT — reasoning-intensive retrieval benchmark (arXiv:2407.12883; ICLR 2025; Su et al.).**
What it is: 1,384 real-world queries across 12 domains where query-document relevance requires multi-step reasoning, not lexical or semantic match.
Take from it: the central number for MelodyScribe's embedder debate: the top MTEB model (59.0 nDCG@10) collapses to 18.3 on BRIGHT, while explicit query reasoning adds up to 12.2 points and top-retriever documents boost QA by 6.6+ points. This is the evidence that on hard queries the harness's reasoning step, not the embedder, is the lever.
Be careful: BRIGHT measures retrieval with an LLM reasoner in the loop; a 2B extractor's reasoning budget is far smaller than the models tested, so the +12.2 is an upper bound, not a promise. Do not use BRIGHT as an ingest-routing benchmark; it has no store-routing decisions.

**4. CodeRAG-Bench — retrieval-augmented code generation benchmark (arXiv:2406.14497; NAACL 2025 Findings; Wang, Asai, Yu, Xu, Xie, Neubig, Fried; code https://github.com/code-rag-bench/code-rag-bench).**
What it is: ~9K coding tasks with 25M retrieval documents from five sources (solutions, tutorials, library docs, StackOverflow, GitHub), canonical-document annotations, and paired retrieval plus execution-based end-to-end evals over 10 retrievers and 10 generators.
Take from it: the methodology MelodyScribe's code-graph store needs: score the retriever (did it fetch the canonical doc) separately from the generator (did it use the context), because the paper shows both fail independently — retrievers on low-overlap queries, generators on integrating context. Canonical docs give GPT-4o +27.4% on SWE-bench and +6.9% on HumanEval in their setup.
Be careful: canonical-document annotation is manual and expensive; MelodyScribe would need Proof or human labels as the canonical-doc oracle. Star count (172) comes from a search excerpt, not the API, and is stale by construction.

**5. tau-bench — tool-agent-user benchmark with consistency metric (arXiv:2406.12045; ICLR 2025; Yao, Shinn, Razavi, Narasimhan; code https://github.com/sierra-research/tau-bench, 1433 stars, MIT).**
What it is: retail and airline domains with realistic databases/APIs, policy documents, LM-simulated users, DB-state-match grading, and a pass^k metric (success over k repeated trials).
Take from it: two harness-evaluation ideas with no equivalent elsewhere in this lane: grading by final database state instead of trajectory match (any valid op sequence scores, which fits Proof's idempotent op keys), and pass^k for consistency — GPT-4o scores <50% pass^1 and <25% pass^8 in retail, with 25% of failures being policy-contradicting decisions and 22% wrong information. MelodyScribe's Proof pass rate should be reported pass^k-style, not single-trial.
Be careful: the user simulator is GPT-4, so difficulty is partly simulator-shaped; open-source models lag proprietary ones here, so small-model numbers will look worse than the headline figures.

**6. MemBench — multi-metric memory benchmark (arXiv:2506.21605; ACL 2025 Findings; Tan, Zhang, Ma, Chen, Dai, Dong; project https://github.com/import-myself/Membench).**
What it is: factual plus reflective memory over participation (agent interacts) and observation (agent watches) scenarios, scored on effectiveness, efficiency and capacity, with seven memory mechanisms implemented on Qwen2.5-7B via MemEngine.
Take from it: the only benchmark in this lane scoring efficiency and capacity alongside accuracy — the direct precedent for MelodyScribe reporting Proof-pass and recall jointly with tokens/s and slot usage. The participation/observation split maps onto MelodyScribe's two ingest paths (live frontier stream vs bulk text).
Be careful: the efficiency/capacity metrics are new and unvalidated elsewhere; treat them as a template, not a standard. Reflective-memory scoring is LLM-judge-based.

**7. BFCL — Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (PMLR 267, ICML 2025; Patil, Mao, Yan, Ji, Suresh, Stoica, Gonzalez; no arXiv version; leaderboard https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html; code in https://github.com/ShishirPatil/gorilla).**
What it is: the de-facto tool-call benchmark: thousands of functions across Python/Java/JS/REST/SQL graded by AST structural match (no execution needed), now spanning single-turn, multi-turn, stateful agentic splits plus cost and latency columns.
Take from it: AST-match grading is the proven way to score grammar-constrained op emission at scale without executing stores, and the headline finding mirrors MelodyScribe's spike results: SOTA models ace single-turn calls but fail memory, long-horizon reasoning and abstention. The irrelevance/relevance splits are the direct analogue of "emit no op" decisions.
Be careful: BFCL tools are stateless APIs, not typed store writes, so it cannot score routing-to-store decisions or idempotency. Contamination handling (live/user-contributed splits in v2+) means scores across versions are not comparable.

**8. ToolSandbox — stateful conversational tool-use benchmark (arXiv:2408.04682; NAACL 2025 Findings; Lu et al.; code https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ToolSandbox).**
What it is: 1,032 expert-authored cases over 34 stateful tools (44% stateful, implicit dependencies like cellular-service-before-message), a GPT-4o user simulator for on-policy rollouts, and milestone/minefield scoring of intermediate and final states over any trajectory.
Take from it: the milestone/minefield design is the best fit for scoring MelodyScribe ingest trajectories: partial credit for correct intermediate store writes and penalties for forbidden ones, without requiring one golden op sequence. Its comparison table (BFCL/ToolEval/API-Bank/ToolTalk/tau-bench on state-dependency, conversational, interactive axes) is the cleanest map of what each harness benchmark actually tests. Hardest categories — state dependency, canonicalization, insufficient-information — match Proof's failure modes.
Be careful: the paper's Table 1 marks tau-bench non-interactive while tau-bench claims dynamic interaction; the two use different definitions (see Unresolved U2). Top score (GPT-4o, 73.0% similarity) is simulator- and judge-sensitive.

**9. ReAct — reasoning-plus-acting harness pattern (arXiv:2210.03629; ICLR 2023 oral top 5%; Yao et al.).**
What it is: the Thought/Action/Observation interleave that founded the modern agent harness, evaluated on HotpotQA/FEVER with a Wikipedia API and on ALFWorld/WebShop (+34pp and +10pp absolute over imitation/RL baselines with 1-2 shots).
Take from it: the empirical case that a thin harness loop (reason to pick the next retrieval, act to ground reasoning) beats both reasoning-only and acting-only designs, and that the loop's trajectories are themselves the training data — the paper explicitly notes finetuning on ReAct traces as future work, which the R1 line later executed.
Be careful: all results are large-model prompting (PaLM-540B); nothing here transfers directly to a 2B grammar-constrained extractor except the loop structure. Few-shot-only; no reward design.

**10. Reflexion — verbal reinforcement without weight updates (arXiv:2303.11366; NeurIPS 2023; Shinn et al.; code https://github.com/noahshinn/reflexion, 3264 stars, MIT).**
What it is: Actor/Evaluator/Self-Reflection loop writing verbal self-critiques into an episodic memory buffer reused next trial; 91% HumanEval pass@1 over a GPT-4 80% baseline, with ablations over feedback type and source.
Take from it: the existence proof that harness signals (binary/scalar/LLM feedback) improve an agent with zero gradient steps — MelodyScribe should keep a no-finetune improvement path (episodic reflection text, e.g. Proof failure patterns fed back as context) alongside the RL path, and can use Reflexion-style loops to generate training data before committing to weight updates.
Be careful: gains compound over multiple trials on the same task; single-pass ingest gets less. Self-reflection quality depends on the reflector model, which at 2B scale is unproven.

**11. Gorilla — small-model tool-use finetuning with retriever-aware training (arXiv:2305.15334; NeurIPS 2024; Patil, Zhang, Wang, Gonzalez; repo https://github.com/ShishirPatil/gorilla, 13019 stars, Apache-2.0).**
What it is: a finetuned LLaMA that beats GPT-4 zero-shot on API-call accuracy across APIBench (94 TorchHub + 696 TensorHub + 925 HF calls), with retriever-aware training (RAT) so the model adapts to test-time documentation changes and hallucinates less.
Take from it: the direct precedent for "design the harness first, then fine-tune the model to it": a small finetuned model with a retriever in the harness beats the frontier model prompted without one. RAT is the pattern for MelodyScribe's Folio/skill side: train with the retrieval context distribution the harness will serve at inference.
Be careful: APIs are stateless single calls, far simpler than typed store writes with evidence quotes; the GPT-4 comparison is zero-shot prompting, not GPT-4 finetuned. Stars figure from API search is a live snapshot, not the paper's claim.

**12. DeepSeekMath — GRPO and RLVR origin (arXiv:2402.03300; Shao et al.; code https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-Math).**
What it is: 120B-token math continue-pretraining plus instruction tuning plus RL, introducing Group Relative Policy Optimization (group-sampled baseline, no value model) and the RLVR practice of RL against verifiable rewards; 7B reaches 51.7% MATH without tools.
Take from it: the algorithmic base the whole reward line builds on: GRPO removes the value network (fits one-GPU budgets), and verifiable outcome rewards (answer checkable by a program) replace human preference labels. Proof pass/fail is exactly a verifiable reward in this sense.
Be careful: math answers verify by exact/script check; Proof verdicts verify harness constraints, and a model can game a validator (see Security S1). Star count from search excerpt, not API.

**13. DeepSeek-R1 — pure-RL reasoning with rule-based rewards plus distillation (arXiv:2501.12948; DeepSeek-AI et al.; also Nature 645, 2025 condensed; checkpoints https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1).**
What it is: R1-Zero trained by large-scale RL with no SFT cold start; rule-based accuracy+format rewards, explicitly no neural reward model after observing reward hacking; reasoning behaviors (verification, reflection, longer chains) emerge; the pipeline then distills into 1.5B–70B dense models that inherit the patterns.
Take from it: three transferable results. First, verifiable rewards beat learned reward models for reasoning — MelodyScribe's Proof validator should be the reward, not a learned critic. Second, format rewards (answer in the right tags) train structural compliance, the analogue of grammar-valid op emission. Third, distilling a strong reasoner's traces into small dense models works — the route for getting thinking into the 2B extractor.
Be careful: R1-Zero is 671B-scale with massive RL compute; emergence at that scale does not predict emergence at 2B. Language-mixing and readability regressions required a cold-start fix — expect analogous format regressions when RL-tuning op emission.

**14. Search-R1 — retrieval environment as RL with token masking (arXiv:2503.09516; COLM 2025; Jin et al.; code https://github.com/PeterGriffinJin/Search-R1, 5413 stars, Apache-2.0).**
What it is: the search engine placed inside the RL rollout; the model interleaves reasoning with multi-turn search calls; retrieved tokens are masked from the loss; a plain exact-match outcome reward suffices; +41% (Qwen2.5-7B) and +20% (3B) over RAG baselines on seven QA sets.
Take from it: the two most actionable training details for MelodyScribe: mask every non-model token (retrieved text, and by extension evidence quotes and store readbacks) out of the policy loss, and start with pure outcome reward before adding shaping — the paper's ablations show the minimal design works. Multi-turn retrieval invocation is learnable by RL rather than promptable.
Be careful: EM outcome reward fits factoid QA; MelodyScribe's ingest quality (routing, quote fidelity) has no EM equivalent and needs the staged rewards of R1-Searcher/Memory-R1. Search-engine latency during RL rollouts is unreported.

**15. R1-Searcher — staged retrieval rewards, no cold start (arXiv:2503.05592; Song et al.; code https://github.com/RUCAIBox/R1-Searcher, 725 stars, MIT).**
What it is: two-stage outcome RL on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B (including base models with no instruction tuning): stage 1 rewards retrieval invocation and format with no answer reward; stage 2 rewards answers; retrieval-mask loss; beats GPT-4o-mini-class RAG and generalizes to live web search (+11.4% on Bamboogle over a 32B Search-o1).
Take from it: the staged-reward recipe for MelodyScribe's exact failure profile (spike 003: ops missing or failing Proof): first reward emitting any grammar-valid op and invoking retrieval, then reward correctness — because the 1B-emits-nothing regime means answer-only rewards give no gradient. Base-model RL without cold start matters: the extractor can be RL-shaped without an SFT stage.
Be careful: multi-hop QA only; store-routing and quote-verbatim constraints are stricter than web-search invocation. The follow-up R1-Searcher++ adds an SFT cold start back, suggesting pure-RL-from-base is brittle — see Unresolved U4.

**16. Memory-R1 — RL for memory operations and memory use (arXiv:2508.19828; ACL 2026; Yan et al.; code https://github.com/yansikuan/memory-r1, 127 stars, Apache-2.0).**
What it is: two decoupled RL agents on 3B–14B backbones: a Memory Manager choosing ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP per turn and an Answer Agent doing memory distillation over RAG hits, both trained with PPO/GRPO on EM outcome reward from only 152 QA pairs; tested on LoCoMo, MSC and LongMemEval.
Take from it: the closest existing answer to the lane question: memory-management ops are trainable actions under downstream-QA reward, decoupled training (manager frozen while answer agent trains and vice versa) stabilizes sparse rewards, and 152 pairs suffice — MelodyScribe's op set (route-to-store, quote attach, no-op) maps directly onto ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP plus abstention. This is also the strongest support for the owner's fine-tuning lever: learned memory control beats the Mem0 heuristic baseline.
Be careful: reported gains moved across versions (v2: +48% F1/+69% BLEU-1/+37% judge; v5: +28%/+34%/+30% on LLaMA-3.1-8B) — quote v5 and expect numbers to keep moving. 127 stars and a 2025 repo mean limited independent reproduction.

**17. vLLM/PagedAttention — serving-memory system and reporting template (arXiv:2309.06180; SOSP 2023; Kwon et al.; https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm, 91647 stars, Apache-2.0).**
What it is: block-level KV-cache management with preemptive scheduling; 2–4x throughput at equal latency versus FasterTransformer/Orca, with larger wins on long sequences.
Take from it (lane-10 scope only): the template for credible serving numbers — fixed model and trace, throughput at equal latency, ablations by sequence length. Caching mechanisms themselves belong to lane 6.
Be careful: all measurements are datacenter-GPU serving, not single-laptop-GPU cohabitation of ingest and recall; do not port the multiples to the RTX 3080 rig.

**18. SGLang — structured LM programs with RadixAttention (arXiv:2312.07104; NeurIPS 2024; Zheng et al.; https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang, 35900 stars, Apache-2.0).**
What it is: a frontend (generation/parallelism primitives) plus a runtime with RadixAttention automatic cross-request KV reuse and compressed-FSM structured decoding; up to 6.4x throughput on agent-control, reasoning, RAG and multi-turn-chat programs.
Take from it: two ideas: instruction-first ordering (spike 009) is exactly the kind of shared-prefix structure RadixAttention monetizes automatically, so prefix-reuse should be measured as a cache-hit rate rather than assumed; and constrained decoding belongs in the runtime (compressed FSM), supporting the harness-side grammar filter while explaining its 9x CPU cost as an implementation gap, not a necessity.
Be careful: the 6.4x figure is over unspecialized baselines on multi-call programs; single-pass section processing gains less. Lane 6 owns the caching work; this item is admitted for measurement framing.

**19. Sarathi-Serve — throughput-latency tradeoff scheduler and SLO reporting (arXiv:2403.02310; OSDI 2024; Agrawal et al.).**
What it is: chunked-prefills plus stall-free scheduling; 2.6x capacity (Mistral-7B, 1xA100), 3.7x (Yi-34B, 2xA100), 5.6x (Falcon-180B pipeline) versus vLLM, reported as serving capacity under tail-latency (TBT) SLOs with an explicit TTFT/TBT tradeoff knob (chunk size).
Take from it: the reporting standard MelodyScribe's "caching and parallel workload" requirement needs: capacity at fixed tail latency, TTFT vs TBT split, and priority behavior of bulk ingest versus realtime requests. Chunked-prefill is also the mechanism that would let a long Score section stream without stalling realtime decodes on one card.
Be careful: figures are A100-based; the single-16GB-card regime with 16 saturated decode slots (spike 009) is unmapped in this literature — see Unresolved U1.

## (b) Refutations

**R1. Embedder choice does not settle recall quality on hard queries (qualifies brief section 1: durable-index baseline).**
The brief treats the Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B baseline (0.801 MRR) versus adapters as the recall-quality decision. BRIGHT shows the MTEB leader at 59.0 nDCG@10 falling to 18.3 on reasoning-intensive queries, with +12.2 recoverable only by explicit query reasoning (arXiv:2407.12883). For MelodyScribe's hardest queries the embedder is not the ceiling; harness-side reasoning is. Status: refutes the sufficiency claim, not the baseline itself.

**R2. Weight updates are not the only learning lever (qualifies "fine-tuning, not scale, is the lever").**
Reflexion improves agents substantially with zero gradient steps via verbal feedback in episodic memory (arXiv:2303.11366), while Memory-R1 improves them via PPO/GRPO weight updates from 152 pairs (arXiv:2508.19828). The brief's lever ranking omits the no-finetune path, which is cheaper to try first and generates the traces RL later needs.

**R3. Outcome-only rewards are insufficient where nothing is emitted (qualifies naive RLVR transfer).**
Search-R1's plain-EM-reward success (arXiv:2503.09516) does not transfer to the 1B-emits-no-ops regime (spike 003): R1-Searcher needs a stage-1 retrieval/format reward with no answer component before answer rewards work (arXiv:2503.05592). Any MelodyScribe reward plan that starts at downstream answer quality will give zero gradient on sections where the extractor emits nothing.

**R4. Harnesses are not compared "in theory before a model is trained" anywhere in this literature (refutes the lane question's presupposition).**
Every harness comparison found — LoCoMo/LongMemEval leaderboards, tau-bench pass^k, ToolSandbox milestones, BFCL splits — is empirical, post-hoc, and model-conditional. No paper derives a pre-training analytic ranking of harnesses. The honest theoretical procedure the literature supports is: fix the model, ablate the harness, report per-capability deltas with consistency statistics (see Implication 8).

## (c) Unresolved ledger

**U1. Single 16GB-laptop-GPU serving numbers for cohabited ingest+recall.** vLLM, SGLang and Sarathi-Serve report on A100/H100-class hardware with serving traces; nothing found reports capacity-under-SLO for one model process serving embedding plus grammar-constrained generation on a 16GB laptop card. Reason: hardware/ workload mismatch; MelodyScribe must measure its own rig.

**U2. What "interactive" means across tau-bench and ToolSandbox.** ToolSandbox's comparison table marks tau-bench non-interactive while tau-bench's paper claims dynamic multi-turn user interaction. Reason: two sources use different definitions (trajectory-freedom vs multi-turn); both admitted, disagreement recorded, no adjudication attempted.

**U3. Memory-R1 number drift across versions.** v2 vs v5 report different relative gains on the same backbone. Reason: single paper, moving versions; v5 figures used throughout, reproduction by others unfound.

**U4. Pure-RL-from-base versus cold-start-first for small models.** R1-Searcher trains from base with no SFT; its successor (R1-Searcher++, unadmitted — abs unopened) reportedly reintroduces SFT cold start, and DeepSeek-R1 needed cold start for readability. Reason: the follow-up primary source was not opened; the 2B-regime answer is genuinely unknown.

**U5. BFCL filed without an arXiv id.** No arXiv version exists; the PMLR page is the primary source and the PDF came from the PMLR-linked file. Reason: brief's file-naming rule assumes arXiv; deviation documented in method.md.

**U6. Star/push figures for five repos are excerpt- or snapshot-grade.** CodeRAG-Bench (172) and DeepSeek-Math (3440) stars come from search excerpts, not the API; all API-fetched figures are live snapshots at fetch time. Reason: GitHub core quota exhausted mid-lane; recorded rather than invented.

**U7. No 2025+ confirmation for ReAct/Reflexion small-model transfer.** Both are large-model prompting papers; their use as 2B-harness evidence is structural analogy, not measured transfer. Reason: no primary source found testing these loops at 1–3B with grammar constraints.

## (d) Security findings (stated as requirements)

**S1. The validator-as-reward must be game-resistant.** DeepSeek-R1 abandoned neural reward models after observing reward hacking during large-scale RL, switching to rule-based rewards (arXiv:2501.12948). Requirement: Proof must include hack-probes (paraphrase-near-miss quotes, date-shifted facts, plausible-but-absent evidence) in its own eval, and any RL stage must report reward-hack checks, not just reward curves.

**S2. Persistent-memory admission needs an attack eval.** This lane's benchmarks assume benign histories, but a harness that writes every frontier-stream claim into memory inherits poisoning risk (lane 8: PoisonedRAG/AgentPoison/MINJA class). Requirement: MelodyScribe's harness eval must include an adversarial write split (contradictory updates, injected instructions in stored text) with a contradiction-handling score; LongMemEval's knowledge-update and abstention splits are the scaffold (arXiv:2410.10813).

**S3. Abstention and policy-adherence are safety metrics, not just quality metrics.** tau-bench finds 25% of failures are policy-contradicting decisions (arXiv:2406.12045); BFCL grades irrelevance detection explicitly; ToolSandbox's insufficient-information category punishes hallucinated tool use (arXiv:2408.04682). Requirement: report abstention rate, irrelevance-detection accuracy and policy-violation rate on every harness variant; never report recall without them.

**S4. Simulator-graded evals must be spot-checked by humans.** tau-bench, ToolSandbox and LongMemEval all lean on LM simulators or judges. Requirement: any MelodyScribe harness comparison must human-audit a sample of simulator-graded trajectories and report agreement, or the comparison is simulator-shaped.

## Harness implications

1. Score MelodyScribe recall on LongMemEval's five abilities — extraction, multi-session reasoning, updates, temporal, abstention — rather than a single QA accuracy, because the failure modes (and fixes) differ per ability [2410.10813].
2. Report Proof reliability as tau-bench-style pass^k over repeated trials with DB-state (store-state) matching, since single-trial pass rates hide the sub-25% consistency regime that matters for unattended ingest [2406.12045].
3. Grade op emission with BFCL-style AST structural matching plus explicit irrelevance/abstention splits, separating "right op, right args" from "rightly emitted nothing" [PMLR-v267-patil25a].
4. Give partial trajectory credit with ToolSandbox-style milestones for correct intermediate store writes and minefields for forbidden ones, so harness variants can be ranked without a single golden op sequence [2408.04682].
5. Train rewards in R1-Searcher stages — first reward grammar-valid op emission and retrieval invocation with no answer component, then reward correctness — because outcome-only rewards give zero gradient in the emits-nothing regime [2503.05592]; mask all non-model tokens (evidence quotes, store readbacks) out of the policy loss [2503.09516].
6. Treat memory-write choice (route-to-store, update, delete, no-op) as an RL action rewarded by downstream QA with decoupled manager/answer training, following Memory-R1's 152-pair result, rather than as a hand-tuned heuristic [2508.19828].
7. Use the Proof validator as a rule-based verifiable reward and avoid learned reward models, following the DeepSeek-R1 reward-hacking experience, and distill strong-reasoner traces into the 2B rather than expecting reasoning to emerge at 2B scale [2501.12948][2402.03300].
8. Compare harnesses by fixing the model and ablating the harness with per-capability deltas and consistency statistics, because the literature contains no analytic pre-training harness ranking — theory-first comparison is unsupported, empirical ablation is the method [2402.17753][2410.10813][2406.12045].
9. Report serving as Sarathi-style capacity under a tail-latency SLO with TTFT/TBT split and bulk-vs-realtime priority behavior on the single card, not raw tokens/s, and measure prefix reuse as a cache-hit rate in the SGLang manner [2403.02310][2312.07104].
10. Keep a Reflexion-style no-weight-update loop (verbalized Proof failures reused as context) running beside the RL track, since it improves agents without gradient steps and produces the traces the RL stage will need [2303.11366][2210.03629].
